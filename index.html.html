What is Claude?

Claude is an AI assistant made by Anthropic. Think of it as a very knowledgeable colleague you can chat with — it can help draft emails, summarise documents, answer questions, brainstorm ideas, analyse data, and much more.

It won't replace your expertise, but it can save you significant time on tasks that would otherwise take hours. With Claude Teams, your conversations are private and are not used to train the AI. RAC's data stays within RAC's account.

What You'll Need

  • Your RAC email address (used to log in)
  • A computer, phone, or tablet with internet access
  • Access to claude.ai or the Claude desktop/mobile app
  • An open mind — no coding or technical skills required

Session 1: Getting Started

Your first steps with Claude — from logging in to having a useful conversation.
60–90 minutes All staff No prior AI experience needed

We'll walk through the basics of accessing Claude and understanding the interface:

  • How to log in at claude.ai using your RAC email
  • Installing the desktop app (optional but recommended)
  • The sidebar: your conversation history, search, and settings
  • Starting a new conversation vs continuing an old one
💡 Key Concept

Each new chat starts fresh. Claude remembers some things about you over time (through its memory feature), but a new conversation doesn't automatically carry forward everything from previous chats. You can always search your past conversations from the sidebar.

The best way to learn is by doing. We'll start with some simple, practical examples that everyone can relate to:

✍ Try This Prompt
What are the key things to include in a professional email declining a meeting?
✍ Try This Prompt
Rewrite this paragraph in a more professional tone: [paste your text]
✍ Try This Prompt
What are some team-building activities suitable for a remote NT community?
✍ Try This Prompt
Explain what a Purchase Order is in simple terms for someone new to finance.

📝 Hands-On Exercise

Try 3–4 prompts of your own choosing. Pick something real from your work — a tricky email you need to write, a question you've been meaning to research, or an idea you want to think through.

The quality of what Claude gives you depends on what you ask. Here are the basics of getting good results:

Be Specific

✗ Weak
"Write an email."
✓ Better
"Write a polite email to a supplier letting them know their invoice for quarry materials has been approved and payment will be processed within 14 days."

Give Context

Claude doesn't know what you're working on unless you tell it. Share relevant background — who the audience is, what the purpose is, what tone you need.

Ask for Changes

Your first result won't always be perfect. That's normal. Say things like "make it shorter", "use a friendlier tone", or "add a section about timelines". Think of it as a conversation, not a one-shot request.

Set the Format

Tell Claude how you want the answer: "Give me this as bullet points", "Keep it under 200 words", "Format this as a table".

🏭 RAC Example

Instead of: "Tell me about grants"

Try:

✍ Try This Prompt
I work at Rirratjingu Aboriginal Corporation in Nhulunbuy, NT. We're looking for federal grants related to Indigenous language preservation. Can you outline what's typically required in an application to the NIAA?

Claude has a personal preferences feature that helps it understand who you are and how you like to work. We'll walk everyone through setting this up:

  1. Go to Settings (bottom-left of the sidebar)
  2. Click General, then find the Profile section
  3. Paste the RAC preferences text (provided separately) into the personal preferences box
  4. Click Save

Once set up, Claude will understand RAC's context — our entities, location, cultural context — without you having to explain it every time. You can also add your own role-specific details like "I work in finance" or "I manage the career pathways program".

⚠ Important
  • Claude Teams conversations are not used to train the AI — your data stays within RAC's account
  • Each person's conversations are private to them
  • Use your Teams account for work only — personal Claude accounts have different privacy settings
  • Always review Claude's output before using it — it's a tool, not a decision-maker
  • Don't share passwords, banking details, or highly sensitive personal information

Session 2: Working Smarter

Applying Claude to real work tasks and learning the features that make it genuinely useful day-to-day.
90 minutes Completed Session 1

One of Claude's strongest everyday uses is helping with written communications. We'll practise:

  • Drafting emails with the right tone for different audiences (board, suppliers, community, government)
  • Reviewing and improving your own writing — paste in a draft and ask Claude to tighten it up
  • Creating templates for recurring communications (e.g. grant acquittals, project updates, meeting summaries)
✍ Try This — RAC Scenario
Draft a brief update email to the CEO summarising this month's key activities across your department. Include a section on any issues or risks. Adjust the tone and length until it feels right.

Claude can help you understand complex topics quickly:

  • Summarise a long policy document, report, or set of meeting notes
  • Research a topic and get a plain-language explanation
  • Compare options — e.g. "What are the pros and cons of these two grant programs for RAC?"
  • Prepare briefing notes for meetings

Claude can read documents you upload directly into a conversation. Supported file types include:

PDF, Word (.docx), CSV, Excel (.xlsx), PowerPoint (.pptx), images, and plain text files.

Practical uses:

  • Upload a PDF report and ask Claude to summarise the key findings
  • Upload a spreadsheet and ask Claude to identify trends or anomalies
  • Upload a draft document and ask for feedback on structure, clarity, or grammar
  • Upload an image of a whiteboard or handwritten notes and ask Claude to transcribe them
💡 File Upload Tip

You can drag and drop files directly into the chat, or click the attachment icon. Claude will read the content and you can ask questions about it. For spreadsheets, the Analysis Tool (enabled by your admin) lets Claude run calculations on your data.

Projects let you group related conversations and give Claude persistent background knowledge for a specific topic.

How Projects Work

  1. Create a Project (e.g. "Grant Applications" or "Quarterly Reporting")
  2. Add custom instructions that apply to every conversation in that project
  3. Upload reference documents to the Project's knowledge base
  4. Start new conversations within the Project — Claude already has the context

RAC Project Ideas

  • A "Report Writing" project with RAC's style guide and templates uploaded
  • A "Grants" project with eligibility criteria and past successful applications
  • A "Board Papers" project with formatting requirements and example papers
  • Department-specific projects with relevant policies and procedures
👥 Shared Projects (Teams Feature)

On Claude Teams, Projects can be shared across your team. This means you can build a knowledge base once and everyone benefits. For example, a shared "RAC General" project could contain organisational policies, entity structures, and common templates.

Session 3: Power Features

Going deeper with Claude's more advanced capabilities and learning how RAC is already using AI to build real tools.
90 minutes Completed Sessions 1 & 2

Claude can search the internet in real time to find current information. This is useful when you need up-to-date data that Claude might not have in its training:

  • Current news, policy changes, or government announcements
  • Finding specific information on websites (e.g. grant deadlines, supplier details)
  • Checking facts or getting the latest version of something

Web search is enabled in your Teams account. Just ask a question that requires current information and Claude will search automatically, or you can say "search the web for..."

Claude can create documents for you — not just write text in the chat, but produce actual downloadable files:

  • Word documents (.docx) with proper formatting, headings, and tables
  • Spreadsheets (.xlsx) with formulas and structured data
  • Presentations (.pptx) with slides and content
  • PDFs, CSVs, and other formats
  • HTML pages, dashboards, and interactive components
✍ Try This Prompt
Create a one-page project status report template for RAC with sections for Project Name, Date, Summary, Key Milestones, Risks, and Next Steps. Format it professionally.

Building on Session 1's basics, here are techniques for getting even better results:

Give Claude a Role

✍ Try This Prompt
You are an experienced grant writer who specialises in Indigenous community development funding. Help me draft the project description section for a language preservation grant.

Provide Examples

Show Claude what good looks like: "Here's an example of the format I need: [paste example]. Now create a similar one for [your topic]."

Break Down Complex Tasks

Instead of asking for everything at once, work through it step by step: "First, outline the structure. Then let's work on each section one at a time."

Ask Claude to Think It Through

"Before you answer, think through the pros and cons of each option and explain your reasoning." This often produces more thoughtful, nuanced responses.

Iterate and Refine

Treat it as a back-and-forth collaboration. Your first prompt gets you started; follow-up prompts refine the result. The conversation is the process.

💡 The Point

None of these required a team of professional developers. They were built by one person using AI as a development partner. AI doesn't just help you write emails — it can help you build solutions to real business problems.

AI tools are evolving rapidly. Some things to watch for:

  • Claude's memory continues to improve — it will get better at remembering your preferences and work context
  • Deeper integrations with tools like Google Drive, allowing Claude to read your documents directly
  • More powerful data analysis capabilities
  • RAC-specific tools and Projects that the whole team can use

  • Start small: Use Claude for one task today. Tomorrow, try another. Build the habit gradually.
  • Always review: Claude is a powerful first draft tool, but you are the expert. Check its work.
  • Share what works: If you find a great prompt or use case, share it with the team.
  • Don't be afraid to experiment: You can't break anything. If a conversation goes sideways, just start a new one.
  • Ask Duane: If you're stuck or want to explore something more advanced, reach out. That's what he's here for.

Session 4A: Claude CoWork

Putting Claude to work on your computer — end-to-end task execution without coding.
90–120 minutes Non-technical staff Cohort of 10–15
⚠ Prerequisite: This session requires completion of Sessions 1–3. You should be comfortable with Claude chat, Projects, file uploads, and basic prompting.

Up to now, you have been using Claude as a chat interface — you ask, Claude answers, you copy the result. CoWork is fundamentally different. It is Claude acting as a digital teammate that can:

  • Access files directly on your computer
  • Plan and execute multi-step tasks on your behalf
  • Spin up multiple sub-tasks running in parallel
  • Save its outputs directly to your local folders — no copying and pasting required
Claude Chat (what you know)Claude CoWork (what's new)
You upload files manually each timeClaude reads files directly from your computer
One question, one answerClaude plans and executes multiple steps automatically
You copy output from the chatFinished files are saved directly to your folders
Works in Anthropic's cloudWorks with your local file system
Great for thinking and draftingGreat for getting work done end-to-end

CoWork is available inside Claude Desktop. Before your first session:

  1. Download and install Claude Desktop from claude.ai
  2. Open Claude Desktop — you will see three tabs at the top: Chat, CoWork, and Claude Code
  3. Click CoWork to switch to the CoWork interface
  4. Select a working folder — create a dedicated folder (e.g. "RAC CoWork Projects")
  5. That's it — CoWork is now ready to use
⚠ Important — Working Folder

Claude CoWork can only access files inside the working folder you select. Before starting any task, make sure all the files Claude will need are already saved in that folder. Think of it as your project desk — put everything on the desk before asking Claude to start work.

The best way to understand CoWork is to see it handle something that would normally take 30–60 minutes.

📝 Hands-On Exercise — Document Summarisation Pipeline

Save 3–4 PDF or Word documents into your working folder (e.g. a policy document, a report, and meeting notes). Then use this prompt:

✍ Try This Prompt
Read all the documents in this folder and create a single summary document that captures the key points from each one. Save it as RAC-Summary.docx.

Watch how CoWork plans the task, reads each file, and saves the finished document — without you doing anything after the initial prompt.

If you have already built Claude Projects, you can bring these directly into CoWork — giving you persistent context plus local file access:

  1. Open your existing Claude Project in the Chat tab
  2. Click the option to add this Project to CoWork
  3. Select your working folder
  4. CoWork will now follow your Project instructions automatically for every task
🏭 RAC Example — Board Paper Preparation

Set up a Claude Project with RAC's board paper format and style requirements. Add it to CoWork. Drop the raw inputs into your working folder.

✍ Try This Prompt
Using the board paper format from the project instructions, compile these documents into a draft board paper and save it to this folder.

One of CoWork's most powerful features is running multiple tasks simultaneously. Simply describe the parallel tasks and CoWork organises the work automatically:

✍ RAC Example — Quarterly Review Package
I need to prepare the quarterly review package. Please do three things at once: (1) summarise the Q1 program reports from the documents in this folder, (2) create a financial summary table from the Xero export spreadsheet, and (3) draft an executive summary combining both. Save all three as separate files.

CoWork will plan the tasks, assign sub-agents, work through them in parallel, and save all three files — typically in a fraction of the time it would take sequentially.

CoWork is powerful but not always the right tool:

  • Use CoWork when you have files to process, multi-step tasks to execute, or outputs that need to be saved locally.
  • Stick with Chat when you are brainstorming, thinking through problems, having a back-and-forth conversation, or just need a quick answer.

The two tools complement each other — CoWork does the work, Chat does the thinking.

💡 Tip — Start Simple

Your first CoWork tasks should be straightforward. Once you are confident with how it works, gradually increase the complexity. Build up your confidence with the tool first.

Session 4B: Claude Skills & Agents

Building reusable AI workflows that the whole team can use — skill libraries, plugins, and agentic automation.
120 minutes Claude Code users Cohort of 2–5
⚠ Prerequisite: This session requires completion of Sessions 1–3 and comfort with Claude Code. Recommended for technically-inclined staff (2–5 people).

Everything in Sessions 1–3 and 4A is about using Claude — asking it to do things for you. Session 4B is about building with Claude — creating reusable systems that execute your workflows automatically, consistently, and at scale.

  • A great prompt you write once has to be written again next time
  • A Claude Skill encodes that prompt, workflow, and context permanently — you never have to write it again
  • Skills can be shared with the whole team so everyone benefits
  • Skills can be combined to handle complex multi-step processes automatically
💡 Key Concept

Think of it like the difference between cooking a meal versus writing a recipe. Session 4A was cooking. Session 4B is writing recipes that anyone — including Claude — can follow reliably every time.

A Claude Skill is a structured file (created inside Claude Code) that defines a specific workflow — its purpose, the steps it follows, the tools it uses, the format of its output, and any reference materials. Once created, a skill can be invoked by name whenever that workflow is needed.

Skill NameWhat It DoesWho Uses It
Grant Application DrafterTakes a grant URL, extracts requirements, matches against RAC's profile, drafts an applicationCommunity Programs, Leadership
Board Paper FormatterTakes raw inputs and formats into RAC's board paper templateAdministration, Leadership
Financial Anomaly ReportReads a Xero export, identifies anomalies vs prior period, produces a one-page summaryFinance
Program Acquittal WriterConverts outcome data and expenditure into a formatted acquittal narrativeCommunity Programs
Correspondence ProcessorReads correspondence, classifies by type and urgency, drafts response templatesAdministration
Quarterly Review PackageCombines program reports, financial data, and executive summary into a review packageLeadership, all teams

The best skills are built from processes that already exist — your SOPs, proven templates, or best examples of past work.

  3. Claude reads the SOP, understands the workflow, and writes the skill file

  5. Test by invoking the skill

📝 Hands-On Build Exercise

We will build one RAC skill together during the session. Bring an existing SOP or 2–3 examples of a common work output (a report, a grant section, a correspondence template). By the end of the session you will have a working skill that any team member can invoke.

The real power of a skill library emerges when skills are used together. A quarterly review workflow might use three skills:

  1. Program Report Summariser — processes all team reports
  2. Financial Anomaly Report — processes the Xero data
  3. Quarterly Review Package — combines both into the final document

Claude orchestrates these automatically. You give it the goal; it plans the work, assigns each skill, runs them in parallel where possible, and delivers the finished package. What might take a full day of manual work becomes a 15-minute automated pipeline.

Once you have a skill library, you can package all skills into a Plugin — a single bundle shared with any team member:

  1. Prompt Claude to package your RAC skills into a plugin
  2. Claude reads your skills and creates the plugin format
  3. Share the plugin zip file with team members
  4. Team members import via Add Plugin — one click and all skills are available

This is how the work you do in Session 4B multiplies across the team. You build once; everyone benefits.

For very complex tasks, Claude can act as a team lead and spin up sub-agents — separate Claude instances each working on a specific part of the problem simultaneously.

✍ RAC Example — Annual Report Preparation
Prepare the annual report package. Task 1: Summarise all program outcome reports from this folder. Task 2: Process the financial statements and create the financial highlights section. Task 3: Draft the CEO's message based on the key themes from Tasks 1 and 2. Run Tasks 1 and 2 in parallel, then complete Task 3 once both are done.
💡 Sub-Agents vs CoWork

Sub-agents are most useful for complex tasks with clearly parallel components where outputs need combining. For most day-to-day work, CoWork's built-in parallelism (Session 4A) is simpler and sufficient. Sub-agents in Claude Code give you more control for automated pipelines.

Every skill you build is a permanent asset for RAC. Some principles for building a skill library that lasts:

  • Start with your highest-frequency, highest-effort tasks — the ones that eat the most time
  • Always base skills on real RAC processes — SOPs, templates, or proven examples
  • Include RAC's context files in every skill so outputs are always on-brand and culturally appropriate
  • Review and refine skills based on team feedback — treat them as living documents
  • Version control your skills folder so changes are tracked over time
